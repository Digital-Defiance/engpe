\begin{abstract}
Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE) enables privacy-preserving genomic diagnostics,
but practical edge deployment requires a clear accounting of which costs are
cryptographic and which are merely arithmetic. We present \engpe{} (Edge-Native
Genomic Privacy Engine), an RNS-CKKS polygenic risk score (PRS) stack for Apple
Silicon (M4 Max) that exposes two deliberately separated pipelines. The
\textbf{Ring Evaluation Engine} performs CKKS encode~/~negacyclic NTT~/~CRT~/~decode
over plaintext ring elements and, at polynomial degree $N = 16384$ and scale
$\Delta = 2^{40}$, amortizes a 110{,}000-SNP evaluation to \textbf{13.7\,ms per
patient} at its best operating point ($M = 16$, Metal GPU lane). The
\textbf{Cryptographic FHE Engine} adds true RLWE KeyGen, encryption of genotypes
\textit{and} weights, ciphertext$\times$ciphertext multiplication with
relinearization, and hybrid Galois key-switching with a fused Metal Digit-NTT
$\to$ KS-MAC $\to$ INTT command buffer, under a \textbf{HEPRS-matched modulus
budget} of seven Metal-eligible limbs ($|Q| = 217$ bits at $N = 8192$, inside the
HE Standard ceiling of 218). An offline Tauri clinic application exercises the
stack without network I/O.

Against the HE-PRS baseline of Knight~\textit{et~al.}~\cite{knight2026heprs}, we
match algorithm structure \textbf{and} the $\log_2(QP)\approx 202$ depth budget
(Metal-compatible primes; see Section~\ref{sec:security} and
\file{PARAMETERS.md}), and measure on the same machine as a local rebuild of their
MIT example (10{,}001 features $\times$ 50 individuals, $N = 8192$). Their
\code{PN13QP202pq} build on this M4 Max finishes in \textbf{1.41\,s}; warm
\engpe{} finishes the same panel with fidelity at file-rounding
($r = 0.999999999$; Section~\ref{sec:crossval}). On-device memory remains the
deployment claim: streamed patient packing keeps $\sim$1{,}146-patient cohorts near
\textbf{0.4--1.4\,GB} rather than materialising $\sim$9\,GB.

Every claim is backed by an executable gate. The audit that produced this revision
found and fixed a silent evaluation-key aliasing defect
(Section~\ref{sec:fused}), a schoolbook $O(N^2)$ ring multiply that accounted for
90\% of encrypted runtime (Section~\ref{sec:encrypted-time}), a rotate-and-sum that
was incorrectly applied once per ciphertext rather than once per individual
(Section~\ref{sec:hoist}), and an i128 overflow in relinearization-key generation
that silently destroyed ct$\times$ct precision at production degrees.
\end{abstract}

%==============================================================================
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Polygenic Risk Scores (PRS) aggregate genotype dosages across hundreds of
thousands of Single Nucleotide Polymorphisms (SNPs). Clinical use is constrained
by privacy: sending raw genotypes to a remote server is often unacceptable under
regulatory and ethical constraints.

CKKS-style approximate arithmetic can evaluate a PRS as a slot-wise product
followed by a horizontal sum. Two layers must not be conflated:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Ring evaluation} --- canonical embedding, negacyclic NTT multiply,
        CRT recombination, and decode --- which validates the arithmetic engine and
        saturates Metal batch NTTs over unified memory.
  \item \textbf{True FHE} --- RLWE ciphertexts $(c_0, c_1)$ under a ternary secret,
        with noise, and with key-switching after every Galois automorphism used in
        rotate-and-sum.
\end{enumerate}

Prior drafts of this work incorrectly attributed ring-engine throughput to
``FHE'' and extrapolated unverified cohort wall-clock times. This revision reports
only measured figures and names the two modes explicitly.

\paragraph{Prior work.}
Knight, Li, Jensen, Gerstein~\textit{et~al.}~\cite{knight2026heprs} demonstrated
end-to-end homomorphically encrypted PRS on real clinical data, evaluating a
110{,}000-SNP schizophrenia model across a $\sim$1{,}100-patient cohort in
approximately six minutes using $\sim$65\,GB of memory for the core PRS
calculation. That result established the clinical viability of HE-PRS and is the
baseline against which \engpe{} should be read. \engpe{} targets a different point
in the design space --- a single consumer device rather than a large-memory server
--- and Section~\ref{sec:heprs} compares the two on matched algorithm structure and
on their released example cohort.

\section{System Architecture}
\label{sec:arch}

\engpe{} targets the Apple M4 Max (performance CPU cluster + 40-core Metal GPU,
unified memory). Both pipelines use Metal-eligible RNS primes $q_i < 2^{31}$ with
$q_i \equiv 1 \pmod{2N}$. The \textbf{ring} path keeps four limbs
($|Q|\approx 124$ bits) for throughput. The \textbf{encrypted} path uses
\textbf{seven} limbs ($|Q| = 217$ bits), matching the HEPRS \code{PN13QP202pq}
depth budget under the Metal prime-width constraint; coefficients live in
\code{ethnum::U256}. Flat \code{Float64Array} FFI packs $M$ genotype panels and
$D$ disease weight panels for a single native call.

\subsection{Security parameters and threat model}
\label{sec:security}

Two parameter sets share the same noise model --- uniform ternary $s$,
$\mathrm{CBD}(\eta = 20)$ so $\sigma \approx 3.16$ --- and differ only in limb
count:

Lattigo's \code{PN13QP202pq} uses $\log_2(QP)\approx 202$ with some primes above
$2^{31}$, which Metal NTT kernels reject. We therefore match the \textbf{budget},
not the bit-identical prime list (\file{PARAMETERS.md}, gate
\longcode{fhe_basis_matches_heprs_qp_budget}). At $N = 8192$, $|Q| = 217$ sits
\textbf{one bit inside} the classical 128-bit ceiling of 218; we report the
envelope rather than a lattice-estimator $\lambda$.

Reduced-degree configurations with a too-large modulus relative to $N$ remain
rejected by \code{require\_secure\_degree}. An audit gate asserts production sets
are inside the envelope and that reduced degrees are flagged outside it.

\paragraph{Threat model.}
This matters because \engpe{} runs every stage on one device, and FHE buys nothing
against an adversary who already holds the key. Both genotypes and model weights
are encrypted under the clinic's public key ($\mathrm{ct}\times\mathrm{ct}$ with
relinearization), matching the cryptographic shape of~\cite{knight2026heprs}'s
evaluator path, while the deployment remains on-device rather than three-party. The
adversary modelled is a compromised or observed \textit{evaluation host} that sees
only ciphertexts; the clinic holds the secret key. The contribution is therefore
that the evaluator role, which in~\cite{knight2026heprs} requires a $\sim$65\,GB
server, fits in under 1\,GB on hardware the data owner can physically control.
Evaluation keys (Galois + relinearization) are memoised per degree for the process
lifetime, so all patients handled by one process share an RLWE key --- acceptable
on-device, not acceptable in a multi-tenant deployment.

%------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Ring Evaluation Engine (plaintext CKKS math)}
\label{sec:ring}

Host Rayon threads encode real slots via the canonical embedding and
CRT-decompose coefficients into four limbs. Each limb is multiplied with a
negacyclic NTT (Metal when available, else CPU). Products are recombined and
horizontally summed (Galois rotate-and-sum on the wide residue polynomial), then
decoded. Multi-disease SIMD broadcasts one patient genotype across $D$ plaintext
weight vectors in the transform domain without re-encoding the genotype.

%------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Cryptographic FHE Engine (true RLWE)}
\label{sec:fhe}

The FHE path adds:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a CSPRNG (\code{getrandom}) for keys and noise;
  \item ternary secret keys $s \leftarrow \{-1,0,1\}^N$;
  \item centered-binomial error $\mathrm{CBD}(\eta=20)$ ($\sigma \approx 3.16$);
  \item uniform $a \leftarrow U(R_Q)$;
  \item public-key encryption to 2-element ciphertexts $(c_0, c_1)$ for both
        genotypes and weights;
  \item ciphertext$\times$ciphertext multiplication with digit-decomposed
        relinearization of $s^2$ (HEPRS \code{MulRelinNew});
  \item hybrid digit-decomposition Galois evaluation keys and fused Metal
        key-switching after each rotate-and-sum automorphism
        (Section~\ref{sec:ks}).
\end{itemize}

%------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{CRT recombination optimization}
\label{sec:crt}

Profiling showed CRT recombination consuming the majority of ring-engine wall time
when implemented as bit-serial double-and-add modular multiplication ($\sim$673\,ms
to recombine one degree-$16384$ polynomial). The present implementation uses
128-bit widening multiply, reduction via precomputed $2^{128} \bmod Q$, and
precomputed CRT weights $w_i = (Q_i \cdot \hat{y}_i) \bmod Q$, with coefficients
recombined in parallel. The \code{crt-recombine} microbench in
\file{results-v3.txt} reports a median of \textbf{0.82\,ms} at $N = 16384$
(min 0.62\,ms, max 2.31\,ms), inside the 10\,ms engineering target.

%------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Asynchronous double-buffering}
\label{sec:async}

A bounded \code{sync\_channel(1)} overlaps host encode+CRT preparation of cohort
$N{+}1$ with limb NTT evaluation of cohort $N$. Correctness is gated by
bit-identical comparison (\code{f64::to\_bits}) against the synchronous path. After
the CRT fix, host prep is a small fraction of total time; overlap remains correct
but yields limited additional throughput on the ring engine.

\section{Galois Key-Switching: Hybrid Digits and Fused Metal Dispatch}
\label{sec:ks}

Horizontal summation in CKKS applies $\log_2(N/2)$ automorphisms
$\varphi_k\colon X \mapsto X^k$ with $k = 5^{2^i} \bmod 2N$. For $N = 16384$ this
is \textbf{13} steps. On a ciphertext $(c_0, c_1)$ encrypted under $s$,
$\varphi_k$ produces a ciphertext under $\varphi_k(s)$. Returning to basis $s$
requires a key-switch: decompose $c_1$ into base-$B$ digits and multiply by
evaluation-key components that encrypt $B^j \cdot \varphi_k(s)$.

%------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Hybrid digit width and auxiliary modulus \texorpdfstring{$P$}{P}}
\label{sec:hybrid}

Phase~5 widens the gadget base from $B = 2^{10}$ (13 digits at
$\|Q\|\approx 124$ bits) to \textbf{$B = 2^{20}$ (7 digits)}, cutting the number
of digit NTTs and EVK MACs per rotation by nearly half. An auxiliary
Metal-eligible special modulus $P$ (one prime $p < 2^{31}$, co-prime to all
$q_i$) is generated alongside evaluation keys for hybrid modulus-raising~/~mod-down.
Exact RNS mod-down $y_i = (x_i - x_P)\cdot P^{-1} \bmod q_i$ is implemented and
gated, and a \code{mod\_down\_batch} Metal kernel scales residues by $P^{-1}$.

To be explicit rather than let a reader infer otherwise: \textbf{the extended $PQ$
hybrid path is built and tested but not engaged in production.} The shipped FHE
circuit already runs under a HEPRS-matched $|Q| = 217$-bit Metal basis
(\code{FHE\_RNS\_LIMBS = 7}, \code{U256} residues); Stage-D mod-down remains
infrastructure for deeper circuits, not a component of the reported latencies. The
auxiliary modulus is generated with every evaluation-key set and its correctness is
gated.

The wider gadget is not free, and the cost is stated here rather than left
implicit. Key-switch noise grows linearly in $B$, so moving from $2^{10}$ to
$2^{20}$ raises it by about $2^{10}$. Measured directly by encrypting zero and
reading the decrypted phase
(\code{audit\_keyswitch\_noise\_budget\_is\_measured}), at $N = 1024$ and
$\|Q\| \approx 124$ bits the fresh-ciphertext noise is $\approx 2^{8}$ and the
post-key-switch noise is $\approx 2^{28}$ --- exactly the predicted factor of $B$.
Relative to the working scale this is $\approx 1.3\times 10^{-4}$ at
$\Delta = 2^{40}$ but only $\approx 1.3\times 10^{-16}$ at $\Delta^{2} = 2^{80}$.
The PRS circuit key-switches \textit{after} the ciphertext$\times$ciphertext
multiply (and relinearization), i.e.\ at $\Delta^{2}$, so the wider gadget is
absorbed with roughly 80 bits of headroom under $Q$. Circuits that rotate at
$\Delta$ rather than $\Delta^{2}$ would need either a narrower $B$ or a larger
$\Delta$; this is a property of the circuit, not of the engine.

A note on rotation semantics, since it is easy to misread the code: slots here are
indexed in the \textbf{natural} embedding order
$\zeta_j = \exp(i\pi(2j+1)/N)$, not the canonical power-of-5 order. Under that
labelling $\varphi_5$ is the permutation
$j \mapsto ((5(2j+1) \bmod 2N) - 1)/2$ folded through conjugate symmetry, which is
\textit{not} a one-position cyclic shift. Rotate-and-sum is unaffected --- it sums
the full orbit, and the PRS dot product is permutation-invariant --- but a reader
expecting a cyclic shift will misjudge the rotation gate. The permutation is
asserted explicitly in
\code{audit\_galois\_keyswitch\_performs\_real\_rotation}.

%------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Fused GPU command buffer}
\label{sec:fused}

Earlier Phase~4 work showed that separate Metal submits for digit NTT, KS MAC, and
INTT \textit{degraded} full-panel latency ($\sim$105\,s) due to sync overhead,
while CPU digit NTT + Metal MAC alone reached $\sim$30\,s. Phase~5 encodes the full
limb pipeline into a \textbf{single \code{MTLCommandBuffer}} with one
\code{waitUntilCompleted}:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Stage A} --- batch forward negacyclic NTT on decomposed digits
        (resident GPU buffer; not read back mid-pipeline).
  \item \textbf{Stage B} --- \code{keyswitch\_mac\_batch} against resident EVK
        \code{MTLBuffer}s (both $b$ and $a$ components).
  \item \textbf{Stage C} --- inverse NTT on each accumulator.
  \item \textbf{Stage D} --- optional \code{mod\_down\_batch} when operating on the
        extended $PQ$ basis.
\end{enumerate}

Galois EVKs remain NTT-transformed once at cold start and held resident on both
host and GPU. Cache entries are keyed by
$(N, k, \mathrm{fingerprint}, \mathrm{limb}, \mathrm{component})$, where the
fingerprint is an FNV-1a digest of the evaluation-key material itself.

The key identity matters. An earlier revision keyed only on
$(N, k, \mathrm{limb}, \mathrm{component})$, so two \textit{distinct} Galois keys
sharing a degree and rotation step aliased to the same cache entry: the second
key-switched under the first key's evaluation material and returned garbage with no
error raised. The precise blast radius is worth stating, because it is narrower
than it first appears and we would rather understate it. The shipped evaluation path
memoises one \code{EvaluationKeys} per degree in a process-global cache
(\code{eval\_keys\_cached}) and never regenerates it, so the current clinic flow
only ever holds one key set per $N$ and does not trigger the alias. What did
trigger it was the test suite, where independent gates each generate their own keys
--- four gates failed together while every one of them passed in isolation, which
is how the defect surfaced. It is also reachable by any consumer of the public
\code{crypto} API that generates its own keys, and it would become reachable in
production the moment per-session or per-patient re-keying is introduced, which is
a natural next step for this system. A silent wrong-answer failure mode that is
latent rather than active is still a publication blocker.

The fix makes the alias unrepresentable rather than merely unlikely, and
\longcode{audit_distinct_galois_keys_do_not_alias_in_evk_cache} key-switches
under three independent key sets on both backends as a regression gate. The cache
is now bounded and flushed on both host and GPU when full, so re-keying cannot grow
resident \code{MTLBuffer} memory without limit. Bit-identical gates require Metal
fused KS to match the CPU oracle on $c_0$/$c_1$ limbs before encrypted evaluation
proceeds.

One related property should be explicit for a security reader: because evaluation
keys are memoised per degree for the lifetime of the process, every patient
evaluated by a given process is handled under the \textit{same} RLWE key. For
on-device edge evaluation, where the data owner also holds the key, this is a
reasonable default. It would not be acceptable in a multi-tenant or server-side
deployment, and the fingerprinted cache is a prerequisite for making per-session
keys safe.

\subsection{Where the encrypted time actually went}
\label{sec:encrypted-time}

The encrypted path was profiled rather than assumed, and the result redirected the
entire optimisation effort. Instrumenting the key-switch phases attributed only
\textbf{4.3\%} of warm runtime to the fused Metal pipeline and left 91\%
unaccounted. The cause was \code{poly\_mul\_as}, the ring multiply behind encrypt,
decrypt, and Galois key generation, implemented as a schoolbook $O(N^2)$ expansion
over \code{u128} --- roughly $1.8\times10^{8}$ modular operations per call at
$N = 16384$. Measured primitives confirmed it exactly: 14 encrypt+decrypt pairs
predicted 19.5\,s against 19.7\,s observed, and 13 Galois keys predicted 42.2\,s
against the 45.2\,s observed setup.

(bit-exact against the schoolbook oracle, gated by
\code{ring\_multiply\_ntt\_matches\_schoolbook}) produced:

  & prefer patient-packed for memory \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

The \textbf{bold row} is the controlled comparison: identical published inputs,
same machine, same $N$, matched crypto job \textbf{and} matched modulus budget.
HEPRS remains faster on that panel; \engpe{} pays for Metal-wide limbs

Fidelity is in Section~\ref{sec:crossval}.

%------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{One rotate-and-sum per patient, not one per ciphertext}
\label{sec:hoist}

Reading Algorithm~1 of~\cite{knight2026heprs} against an earlier draft exposed a
plain inefficiency. HEPRS multiplies each of the $K$ genotype ciphertexts by its
weight block, \textbf{accumulates them all first}, and calls \code{InnerSumLog()}
once --- $\log_2(N/2)$ rotations per individual. The earlier \engpe{} draft folded
every ciphertext, costing $K\log_2(N/2)$ rotations.

At production that was $14\times 13 =$~\textbf{182} rotation key-switches per
patient versus HEPRS's \textbf{13}. Hoisting is semantics-preserving
(\code{score\_encrypted\_patient\_disease} in \file{ckks\_rns.rs}). With
ct$\times$ct, each of the 14 products also needs a relinearization key-switch, so
the counted cost is \textbf{27 key-switches per patient} (14 relin + 13 rotate)
rather than 13 --- still far below the pre-hoist 182 rotations, and the structure
of the fold matches~\cite{knight2026heprs}.

%------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Packing as a tunable frontier}
\label{sec:packing}

Transposing the layout so that slot $p$ carries patient $p$ turns the PRS into
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:patient-pack}
\mathrm{acc} \;=\; \sum_i \mathrm{Enc}(g_{\cdot,i}) \cdot w_i
\end{equation}
with $w_i$ a scalar broadcast across slots, so slot $p$ of the accumulator is
patient $p$'s score. There is no horizontal sum: the rotation count is
\textbf{exactly zero} and Galois keys are not needed at all. We implemented this
(\file{native/src/packing.rs}) and gated it against the plaintext oracle for every
patient.

Measured per-ciphertext primitives at $N = 16384$ under the earlier ct$\times$pt
path --- encrypt 57.9\,ms, ct$\times$pt 20.4\,ms --- projected the transposed
layout to $\sim$\textbf{1.05\,s per patient}. That projection is superseded by the
shipped \textbf{patient-packed streamed} evaluator
(\longcode{evaluate_prs_patient_packed_napi}~/~
\longcode{bench_patient_packed_synthetic_napi}): SNPs are encrypted and multiplied
one at a time into a single accumulator, Galois key-switches are \textbf{zero}, and
genotypes need not reside as an $M\times n_{\mathrm{snp}}$ matrix in the
evaluator. On this M4 Max, a synthetic 1{,}146-patient cohort at $N{=}8192$
measures \textbf{$\sim$388\,MiB} RSS at 512 SNPs and \textbf{$\sim$1.4\,GB} at
2{,}000 SNPs (\file{results-packed-memproof.txt}) --- far below the
\textbf{$\sim$9.1\,GB} of a na\"ive SNP-packed materialisation of 1{,}146$\times$10k
(\file{results-cohort-example1146.txt}). Residual RSS growth with SNP count is
dominated by Metal/host caches around relinearization, not by storing the panel;
bounding those caches is follow-on work.

The two layouts remain endpoints of a tunable frontier. A block-packed ciphertext
carrying $P$ patients and $8192/P$ SNPs costs
$\lceil n_{\mathrm{snp}} P / 8192\rceil \cdot \log_2(8192/P) / P$ key-switches per
patient under a \textit{per-ciphertext} fold; with the hoisted fold of
Section~\ref{sec:hoist} the deployed $P = 1$ SNP-packed layout costs
$\log_2(N/2)$ rotations plus one relin per ciphertext. $P = N/2$ recovers the
streamed patient-packed path (zero rotations). Both endpoints and the monotonicity
are asserted in \code{block\_packing\_interpolates\_between\_layouts}.

We suspect the $\sim$65\,GB in~\cite{knight2026heprs} is the cost of materialising
a cohort-packed panel. We rebuilt and timed their public example on this machine
(Section~\ref{sec:heprs}); we have not profiled their 1{,}146-patient clinical path
(controlled-access data). Stage splits for our SNP-packed path (weight encrypt wall
+ patient-eval wall) are exposed via
\code{evaluate\_prs\_encrypted\_staged\_napi}
(\file{results-stages-matched.txt}).

\subsection{Cross-implementation validation on the published HEPRS dataset}

Every correctness figure elsewhere in this paper is measured against \engpe{}'s

\section{Validation Gates}
\label{sec:gates}

Integrity checks include: sparse schoolbook negacyclic convolution gates;
bit-identical async vs.\ sync ring outputs; multi-disease SIMD oracle checks at
$\epsilon < 10^{-4}$; encrypted-path oracle checks; bit-identical Metal vs.\ CPU
Galois key-switch (\longcode{assert_metal_keyswitch_matches_cpu}); and Phase~5
fused hybrid oracle + aux-$P$ mod-down round-trip
(\longcode{assert_fused_hybrid_keyswitch_matches_oracle}). The offline clinic
path sets an explicit air-gap flag and performs no network I/O in the evaluation
core.

A precision gate only shows that the pipeline is \textit{accurate}; it does not
show that the pipeline is \textit{what this paper describes}. A second suite
(\file{native/src/audit\_gates.rs}) targets that gap directly, and each of its
assertions corresponds to a claim made above:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Ciphertexts are RLWE, not encoding.}
        $c_0$ must not equal the encoded plaintext, $c_1$ must be non-zero, two
        encryptions of one message must differ, and a \textit{wrong} secret key
        must fail to recover the message (it does, by $\gg 1$ absolute error).
        Companion gates confirm products remain key-bound (ct$\times$pt and
        ct$\times$ct paths).
  \item \textbf{Fresh noise exists and is bounded.}
        Encrypting zero and decrypting yields the noise directly:
        $\approx 2^{8}$ against a $2^{124}$ modulus. A ciphertext that decrypted
        to exactly zero would indicate encoding rather than encryption.
  \item \textbf{The transform-domain ring multiply is bit-exact.}
        Replacing the schoolbook $O(N^2)$ multiply
        (Section~\ref{sec:encrypted-time}) touched encrypt, decrypt, and key
        generation at once, so it is gated by exact per-coefficient equality
        against the schoolbook oracle across several degrees, plus a check that an
        unregistered modulus still falls back correctly.
  \item \textbf{Parameters stay inside a published security envelope.}
        The production set is asserted inside the HE Standard 128-bit
        ceiling~\cite{hestandard2018}, \textit{and} reduced-degree configurations
        are asserted to be correctly flagged \textbf{outside} it, so a test-only
        lane can never be mistaken for a deployable one
        (Section~\ref{sec:security}).
  \item \textbf{Key-switching performs the real automorphism.}
        Asserted against the explicit $\varphi_5$ slot permutation, and separately
        asserted \textit{not} to be the identity.
  \item \textbf{The fused Metal pipeline actually executes.}
        Atomic counters record fused versus fallback limb key-switches; the gate
        requires every limb to take the fused path \textit{and} to remain
        bit-identical to the CPU oracle.
  \item \textbf{The key-switch count behind Section~\ref{sec:heprs} is measured,
        not derived.}
        After the hoist and the move to ct$\times$ct, the full 110k panel costs
        \textbf{14 relinearizations + 13 rotations = 27 key-switches per patient}
        (108 limb ops at 4 RNS limbs), confirmed by
        \code{audit\_full\_panel\_keyswitch\_count\_matches\_paper}
        (\code{--ignored}).
  \item \textbf{ct$\times$ct + relin is precision-gated through production degree.}
        Unit tests cover single multiplies through $N{=}8192$ and a two-ciphertext
        PRS fold; cross-validation against~\cite{knight2026heprs}'s published
        predictions is Section~\ref{sec:crossval}.
  \item \textbf{Evaluation keys cannot alias.}
        Three independent key sets sharing $(N, k)$, on both backends
        (Section~\ref{sec:fused}).
  \item \textbf{The hybrid gadget is real and $P$ is well formed.}
        Every aux prime is $< 2^{31}$, NTT-eligible, distinct from all $q_i$, and
        $P^{-1} \bmod q_i$ is verified to be a genuine inverse. Digit count scales
        with $\|Q\|$ (11 digits at $\|Q\| = 217$ with 20-bit gadgets).
  \item \textbf{FHE modulus matches the HEPRS QP budget.}
        Seven Metal limbs at $N = 8192$ yield $\|Q\| = 217$ bits, inside the HE
        ceiling of 218 and comparable to Lattigo \code{PN13QP202pq}
        (\code{fhe\_basis\_matches\_heprs\_qp\_budget}).
\end{itemize}

These gates were written as an adversarial pre-publication audit and were not all
green when first run. They surfaced the evaluation-key aliasing defect of
Section~\ref{sec:fused}, a stale key-switch noise budget left behind by the move
to a 20-bit gadget, the reduced-degree security trap of
Section~\ref{sec:security}, the quadratic ring multiply of
Section~\ref{sec:encrypted-time}, and an i128 overflow in relinearization-key
generation that destroyed ct$\times$ct precision at large $N$. The suite is 56+
Rust tests (heavier measurement gates marked \code{\#[ignore]}) and the TypeScript
gates, stable across repeated runs.

\section{Deployment}
\label{sec:deploy}

\engpe{} ships a Tauri desktop ``stateless clinic'' that invokes the native sweep
locally. Synthetic cohorts are generated on-device; ring or encrypted evaluation can
be selected according to the threat model. The UI does not require network access
for a completed sweep.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\engpe{} separates a high-throughput \textbf{ring evaluation} path
(13.7\,ms/patient amortized at its $M{=}16$ optimum for 110k SNPs) from a
correctness-bearing \textbf{RLWE FHE} path that matches HEPRS's cryptographic job
and modulus budget: encrypted genotypes \textit{and} weights, ct$\times$ct with
relinearization, one hoisted rotate-and-sum per individual, and seven Metal limbs
with $\|Q\| = 217$ bits (\textbf{5.41\,s} warm~/~1.11\,GB RSS at 110k$\times$1 on
an M4 Max).

Measured against Knight~\textit{et~al.}~\cite{knight2026heprs} on the same machine
and their MIT example ($N{=}8192$, ct$\times$ct, \textbf{matched $\approx$202-bit
modulus budget}): their \code{PN13QP202pq} build finishes in \textbf{1.41\,s}; warm
\engpe{} finishes in \textbf{$\sim$10.5\,s} ($r = 0.999999999$ vs.\ their preds).
We are not faster. The presentable claim is \textbf{on-device memory +
Metal-compatible parameter parity}: $|Q| = 217$ bits with seven limbs $< 2^{31}$,
and a streamed patient-packed evaluator that keeps $\sim$1{,}146-patient cohorts
near \textbf{0.4--1.4\,GB}. Packed RSS still creeps with SNP count (host/Metal
caches); bounding that is deferred.

Five limitations are worth stating plainly. The GPU advantage on the ring engine
inverts above $M \approx 64$. Evaluation keys are memoised per degree for the
process lifetime. The extended $PQ$~/~Stage-D mod-down machinery is built and gated
but not engaged in production. Correctness against an independent oracle is
Section~\ref{sec:crossval} only; 110k panels are synthetic. No clinical claim is
made: PsychENCODE/PGC data behind~\cite{knight2026heprs} was not used.

Methodologically, profiling redirected this work more than any kernel did: a
quadratic host multiply was 90\% of encrypted runtime; reading their Algorithm~1
cut rotations from 182 to 13 per patient; matching ct$\times$ct exposed an i128
overflow in relin-key generation that precision gates on ct$\times$pt would never
have caught.

The most valuable next experiment is bounding Metal/host caches so patient-packed
RSS stays flat as $n_{\mathrm{snp}}$ grows to 110k, then measuring that path
end-to-end against their 1{,}146-patient server figure under matched stage splits.
Beyond that: keep digit buffers resident across rotations, engage Stage-D mod-down
for deeper circuits, and obtain a bit-precise $\lambda$ from the lattice
estimator.
